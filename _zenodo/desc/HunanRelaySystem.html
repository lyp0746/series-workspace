Abstract.The paper begins with two family memories — a grandfather running boats on the Laodao River in the 1940s–50s, and a father working in Liuyang’s southern district in the 1980s–90s — and uses them to frame a reconstruction of the Ming–Qing relay network of central Hunan. The evolution is organised with the GEPF three-layer model: a slow geographical–demographic layer (G, P), a medium economic layer (E), and a fast fiscal–institutional layer (F), coupled by transport cost, tax base and infrastructure investment and periodically reset by exogenous shocks. Results: 510+ relay stations, 150+ boats and about 3,000 workers; three distinct distance regimes; corridor density that does not reproduce from the 28 key nodes (north 12.8 km, east 16.2 km, west 22.7 km, south 70.6 km; p=0.19), so the density hypothesis is withdrawn pending full station-level geocoding; four intermodal hubs saving 25–33%; an annual expenditure of about 174,850 liang and a trade/expenditure ratio of 114:1 that is not a social benefit–cost ratio; a cross-county toponym-retention regression that is negative but weak (y = 72.3 − 0.89T, R² = 0.58, n = 6, slope CI −1.95 to 0.17), with Ningxiang as an upward outlier; and five phases ending with the 1913 abolition and post-1990 toponym erosion. Limits: four prefectures only, a cross-section rather than a panel, B/C-grade quantities, n=4 for corridor comparison, and family memory as grade-C evidence.
