Abstract.Periodization is not a fact but a cut. On one timeline, Western-civilization textbooks cut at 476, 1500, 1789 and 1914; global-history textbooks at 1000, 1500, 1750 and 1914; Chinese textbooks at 1500, 1640, 1917 and 1945. Among fourteen premodern and non-European chronicles, five print no absolute year at all. This paper codes sixty sources into 252 boundary entries and tests ten pre-registered propositions plus one identification result. Boundary years are concentrated (top ten 69.7% vs 10.1% under a uniform null), but the mechanism is template replication: forty world/regional sources use only 32 distinct top-level cut sets; the three traditions place different gates (global 64% at 1000–1225 and 71% at 1750±15; Chinese 82% at 1640 and 91% at 1917; Eurocentric 13%/7% and 0%/0%); 1500 is shared (73%/71%/64%). The crossing curve is an identity with the marginal distribution (R²=1.0000) and is demoted to a derived display. Partition distance is higher within traditions (0.65) than between (0.51; permutation p=0.0005), yet tradition explains only 29% of variance. 1500 survives but its exclusivity reverses (0.40/1.00/0.14); only 1500 and 1914 travel across all three traditions. Four propositions are not supported: W4 runs backwards, the second half of W5 fails, W6 has direction but no strength, and W9's level component fails (κ=0.53).
