Abstract.The same effort, spent two ways, yields two different results. Under time pressure, sleep debt, and heavy consequences, people do not become lazy: they reallocate a finite bit budget. When the environment is wide they shrink the support and keep a single tunnel; when it is narrow they shrink the resolution and everything blurs. The book starts from one stationarity condition - minimising weighted distortion plus rate cost, v∗=(T+λ)/(2w) - and pushes it to four layers: person, group, society, civilisation. The layers are not a ladder but a graph with loops. Seven parts, thirty-three chapters. It carries derivations and reproducible readings: 21,063 verification records, 45 periodisation schemes, 18 language-pair narrative distances, 124,147 sense entries, 398 polities, 125 calendars, and a 133-person gradient measurement. Every number is graded A/B/C; no conclusion outruns its evidence.

Central claim.A rule is a partition of a state space; understanding is estimating that partition; a resolution sits at the stationarity point between the cost of error and the price of fineness. From this: doubling coverage halves precision; writing the cost of cut points into the ledger gives two scaling laws (a tenfold stake buys the square root or cube root of ten classes); recoding costs once each way, hence asymmetry (bandwidth = 2C). There is one principle, five modelling assumptions and one network convention; P1-P6 are retrieval labels only, read as two principles plus four interfaces.
