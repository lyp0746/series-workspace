ways, yields two different results. Under time pressure, sleep debt and heavy consequences, people do not become lazy: they reallocate a finite bit budget. When the environment is wide they shrink the support and keep a single tunnel; when it is narrow they shrink the resolution and everything blurs. The book starts from one stationarity condition - minimising weighted distortion plus rate cost, v*=(T+λ)/(2w) - and carries it through four layers: person, group, society, civilisation. The layers are not a ladder but a graph with loops: upward, slow variables enter fast layers as parameters (emergence); downward, thresholds rewrite the feasible set of the slow layer (reset). Eight parts, thirty-nine chapters, from a family dinner argument about abandoning an agent to the question of where the book stops. It offers derivations and reproducible readings: 21,063 verification records, 45 periodisation schemes, 18 language-pair narrative distances, 124,147 sense entries, 398 polities, 125 calendars, a 133-person gradient measurement, and 266 section-level revision intervals. Every number is graded A/B/C; no conclusion outruns its evidence.</p><p><b>Central claim.</b>A rule is a partition of a state space; understanding is estimating that partition; a resolution sits at the stationarity point between the cost of error and the price of fineness. From this: doubling coverage halves precision (δ=Δ/2^R); writing the cost of cut points into the ledger gives two scaling laws (a tenfold stake buys the square root or cube root of ten classes); recoding costs once each way, hence asymmetry (bandwidth = 2C).
