Abstract.On 13 August 2026 DeepSeek open-sourced its plugin framework Harness; within a day a new-repository series jumped by an order of magnitude, and ten weeks later a second series around “skills” was still accelerating. The two are often called one ecosystem and treated as a contemporary specimen of community emergence. Using 103 queries against the public GitHub search API (snapshot 2026-10-23), the paper tests that claim with the three markers of the author’s monograph on emergence (order-parameter jump, critical slowing down, macroscopic predictive advantage). Results: (1) the jump aligns exactly with the launch, but the pre-launch baseline is 23.9 repositories per day, not zero, and 651 “pre-launch” repositories are largely retro-tagged; (2) five measurement resolutions over the same month yield two opposite directions, from +62% to −61%, a spread far larger than the data error, with the sign flip located between the ★≥1 and ★≥10 thresholds; (3) the two topic lines share only 414 repositories (Jaccard 0.7%), so “Harness and its companion skills community” does not hold at the label level. The three-marker verdict is zero of three with two cells unmeasurable, i.e. the criterion is inapplicable rather than refuted. Five negative results are reported, and a “measurability gate” is proposed: before applying the markers, ask whether each required quantity can be obtained at all.
